SHAPI is an outcome protection layer for modern software.

Here is the problem. A webhook or API request can return HTTP 200 and still fail to complete the real business action. A payment may be accepted but never activate an account. A deployment may report success while the production outcome is broken. Traditional monitoring sees the response. SHAPI watches the outcome.

This is the SHAPI command center. It connects to providers such as Stripe and GitHub, receives live signals, and checks them against business invariants: simple rules that describe what success must look like.

The overview gives the team one clear answer: what is healthy, what needs attention, and what should happen next. Here we can see two connected sources, seven outcome checks, and three open exceptions.

The protection loop is simple. First, SHAPI receives a signal. Next, it verifies the event against the expected business outcome. When behavior drifts, SHAPI creates a reviewable incident with the integration, severity, evidence, and current state.

The live protection map shows how the system is connected: the API gateway, event mesh, outcome engine, repair queue, and drift monitor. Each node is selectable so a team can move from a high-level status to the exact area that needs review.

SHAPI does not silently change production. It prepares a repair target and a reviewable pull request, while keeping the evidence and required checks visible to the team. That gives operators speed without giving up control.

In one sentence: SHAPI turns invisible business failures into visible, explainable, and safely reviewable repairs.
